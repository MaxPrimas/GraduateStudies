H₂ Molecular Orbitals

Bring two hydrogen 1s orbitals together and they combine in two ways. Added, they pile electron density between the protons and lower the energy: a bond. Subtracted, they leave a node there and raise it.

Geometry

In Bohr radii. 1 a₀ = 0.529 Å.
What is being computed

This is the linear-combination-of-atomic-orbitals (LCAO) picture with one 1s orbital on each proton, φ = e^(−r/a₀)/√(πa₀³):

ψ± = (φ₁ ± φ₂) / √(2(1 ± S))

Everything follows from three integrals, all known in closed form (atomic units, R in a₀, energies in hartree):

overlap S = ⟨φ₁|φ₂⟩ = e^(−R) (1 + R + R²/3)
Coulomb J = ⟨φ₁|−1/r₂|φ₁⟩ = −(1/R) [1 − (1 + R) e^(−2R)]
resonance K = ⟨φ₁|−1/r₁|φ₂⟩ = −(1 + R) e^(−R)
E±(R) = E₁ₛ + (J ± K)/(1 ± S) + 1/R

The last term is the repulsion between the protons. The energies are for a single electron shared by the two protons, which is the H₂⁺ ion: the one-electron problem that the H₂ bond is built from. In this approximation the bonding curve bottoms out at R = 2.49 a₀, 1.76 eV below a separated atom and proton. The exact H₂⁺ answer is 2.00 a₀ and 2.79 eV, so the simple picture gets the bond but underestimates it.

Neutral H₂ puts two electrons in ψ₊. Their mutual repulsion is not included here, which is why the real H₂ bond length (1.40 a₀) is not the minimum of this curve. The antibonding curve has no minimum at all: an electron in ψ₋ pushes the protons apart.
