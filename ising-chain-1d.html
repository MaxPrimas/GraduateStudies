1D Ising Chain

200 spins on a ring, each coupled to its two neighbours and to a field. Cool the chain and the domains grow, but at any temperature above zero they never lock into one: there is no phase transition in one dimension.

What is being computed

Energy of a configuration, with periodic boundaries and s = ±1:

H = −J Σ sᵢ sᵢ₊₁ − h Σ sᵢ

The simulation is single-spin-flip Metropolis Monte Carlo: pick a spin at random, flip it if that lowers the energy, otherwise flip it with probability exp(−ΔE / kBT). One sweep is 200 attempted flips.

The exact results come from the transfer matrix, whose eigenvalues are

λ± = e^K [ cosh b ± √(sinh² b + e^(−4K)) ], K = J/k_BT, b = h/k_BT
m = sinh b / √(sinh² b + e^(−4K))
⟨s₀ sᵣ⟩ − m² = (1 − m²) (λ₋/λ₊)ʳ, ξ = 1 / ln(λ₊/λ₋)

At h = 0 the correlation is tanhʳ K and the density of domain walls is 1/(1 + e^(2K)). The correlation length ξ is finite at every T > 0 and m(h) passes smoothly through zero, so there is no spontaneous magnetisation. ξ diverges only as T → 0.

The measured values are running averages since the last change of T or h. They take longer to settle at low temperature, where the domain walls move slowly, and they drift from the exact curve once ξ approaches the length of the chain.
